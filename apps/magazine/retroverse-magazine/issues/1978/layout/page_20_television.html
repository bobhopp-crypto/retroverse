The Shows Keeping the Week in Sync

Hooks, premises, and the reason each title kept returning to the conversation.

Shows13
Comedy Load4
Networks3
#01ABC

Laverne & Shirley

Working-girl comedy with elbow room.

Physical gags, factory-floor rhythm, and a friendship sturdy enough to carry every scene change.

Why it mattered It kept blue-collar sitcom energy bright without sanding off the grit that made it pop.

#01ABC

Mork & Mindy

Alien velocity in an otherwise normal room.

One performer turns manic improvisation into a full network event the second he enters frame.

Why it mattered It showed how fast a single comic voice could take over the schedule when the timing was right.

#02ABC

Three's Company

Misunderstanding as a weekly athletic event.

A roommate farce where the premise stays simple and the timing does the heavy lifting.

Why it mattered Its breezy pace made network comedy feel easy, even when every beat depended on precision.

#02CBS

WKRP in Cincinnati

Radio panic, finally written by people who understand the booth.

A workplace comedy where DJs, sales headaches, and on-air disasters all move at music-industry speed.

Why it mattered It translated radio-era cool into television form without losing the workplace friction.

#03ABC

Taxi

Night-shift melancholy with punch lines attached.

A dispatch office full of bruised dreamers, every one of them funnier than their circumstances.

Why it mattered It proved character comedy could stay intimate and still feel instantly essential.

#04ABC

Angie

Romance with city grit still on the shoes.

Blue-collar charm and glossy aspiration squeeze into the same half hour.

Why it mattered Its rise showed viewers still wanted warmth, but not without a little class tension in the room.

#04CBS

Dallas

Oil, money, and a grin you should not trust.

Prime-time melodrama turns family dinner into a property dispute with better tailoring.

Why it mattered It announced the appetite for glossy, serialized trouble before the decade was even finished.

#05CBS

60 Minutes

The stopwatch still feels like a starting gun.

A newsmagazine that turned Sunday-night seriousness into appointment television.

Why it mattered It made hard reporting feel like mass-viewing ritual instead of niche homework.

#05NBC

Diff'rent Strokes

A catchphrase with network velocity.

Fish-out-of-water family comedy built to land quick laughs and instant recognition.

Why it mattered It arrived already sounding quotable, which is half the battle on television.

#06CBS

M*A*S*H

Sharp wit, steady pulse, no wasted line.

An ensemble that pivots from sarcasm to ache so quickly it barely looks like a pivot at all.

Why it mattered It remained the standard for television that could be funny, sad, and exact in the same breath.

#07ABC

The Ropers

Domestic side-eye in spin-off form.

Neighborhood comedy powered by suspicion, impatience, and two people never fully sharing the same mood.

Why it mattered It captured television's confidence that a recognizable pair could carry a whole new lane on attitude alone.

#08CBS

All in the Family

Argument as prime-time engine.

A living-room battleground where every small remark lands like a weeklong debate starter.

Why it mattered It proved sitcom form could stay broad and still keep the national conversation uncomfortably close.

#10ABC

Eight Is Enough

Domestic sprawl, handled at full network width.

Family drama where the household is big enough to carry comedy, conflict, and sentiment at once.

Why it mattered It kept the warm, ensemble side of the schedule alive while flashier shows grabbed headlines.
