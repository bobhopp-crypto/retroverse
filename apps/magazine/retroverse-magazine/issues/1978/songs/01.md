# 01. Stayin' Alive — Bee Gees
*By Bob Jones, Music Features Editor*

The opening pulse of "Stayin' Alive" doesn't wait for permission — it takes over the room. That tight, relentless rhythm hits first, locking everything into motion before a single word is sung. By the time the vocal comes in, the floor is already moving.

<blockquote class="pull-quote">
“The rhythm hits first — and it never lets go.”
</blockquote>

It's impossible to separate the song from the scene around it. With Saturday Night Fever driving it into every theater and every club, the record has become the sound of the dance floor itself. You hear it in packed rooms under mirrored balls, in car radios with the windows down, in bars where nobody's sitting still for long.

What makes it work isn't just the beat — it's the precision. The falsetto cuts clean through the track, riding that groove without ever slipping. Every part feels engineered for movement: tight, controlled, and built to keep people on their feet.

And once it starts, it doesn't fade into the background. This isn't music you ignore. It's music that fills the room and demands attention. You don't talk over it — you move with it.

The title says everything. "Stayin' Alive" isn't just a lyric — it's the energy of the year. In a moment defined by nightlife, momentum, and motion, this is the record that captures all of it in three and a half minutes.

It doesn't just sit at the top of the chart — it defines what being there sounds like in 1978.
