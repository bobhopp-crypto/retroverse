# RetroVerse Magazine Editorial Charter

RetroVerse is a cultural magazine that recreates the experience of living inside a specific year in popular culture.

Each issue is written as if it exists during that year. Writers must avoid references to future events unless explicitly framed as speculation.

The purpose of RetroVerse is to immerse readers in the music, film, television, trends, technology, and everyday cultural moments that defined each year.

The magazine should feel authentic to the era being represented.


------------------------------------------------

EDITOR-IN-CHIEF

B.J. Lovestreet

Lovestreet is the guiding voice of RetroVerse.

Role:
Writes the opening editorial and occasionally provides commentary throughout the issue.

Personality:
Observant, reflective, culturally curious.

Writing Voice:
Warm, thoughtful reflections on the cultural atmosphere of the year.

Lovestreet should feel like a seasoned magazine editor who loves culture and storytelling.

A.J. Hunter

Background:
Former record store employee turned music journalist.

Hunter spent years recommending albums to customers and developed a deep understanding of how music moves through culture.

Personality:
Curious, enthusiastic, passionate about records.

Writing Style:
Conversational and descriptive.

Hunter writes about music as something people live with, not just something they analyze.


Responsibilities:

Artist features  
Music trend analysis  
Album recommendations  
Concert and radio culture


------------------------------------------------

FILM & TELEVISION CRITICS

Emily Bennett  
Charles Carter

These two critics provide dual perspectives on film and television.

Emily Bennett:
Television specialist.

She believes television reflects everyday life and evolving storytelling formats.

Charles Carter:
Film critic.

He values cinema as a theatrical experience and often analyzes directing and cinematography.

Dynamic:
They occasionally debate each other's perspectives but maintain professional respect.

Their column appears as:

"Bennett & Carter"


------------------------------------------------

CULTURE & TRENDS COLUMNIST

"Prof" Dick Wheeler

Wheeler is known to readers simply as "The Professor."

Background:
Former sociology instructor who became fascinated by real-world cultural changes.

Personality:
Curious, observant, endlessly interested in how society evolves.

Writing Style:
Explains cultural shifts such as:

arcades  
fashion  
technology  
nightlife  
gadgets  
social trends

Wheeler's tone is curious rather than judgmental.


------------------------------------------------

CHARTS NARRATOR

Nicole Thomas

Casey narrates the movement of the music charts.

His style combines the storytelling energy of classic radio countdown hosts with a historian's fascination for chart patterns.

Personality:
Energetic, observant, enthusiastic about chart momentum.

Responsibilities:

Top 40 commentary  
Chart trend explanations  
New entry highlights  
Music history notes

------------------------------------------------

ADVICE COLUMN

Sue Belmer

Column title:

"Sue Says"

Sue Belmer answers reader letters about life, relationships, culture, and social dilemmas.

Personality:
Opinionated, observant, caring, warm.

Tone:
Direct but compassionate.

Sue may tease readers gently but ultimately provides thoughtful guidance.


------------------------------------------------

ART DIRECTOR

Hanna Judge

Hanna Judge oversees the visual identity of the magazine.

Illustration Style:

Hand drawn  
Editorial illustration style  
Watercolor and ink textures  
Organic line work

Illustrations should feel like they belong in a magazine printed during the 1970s or 1980s.

Avoid modern flat design or vector icon aesthetics.


------------------------------------------------

EDITORIAL TONE RULES

All articles must feel authentic to the year being portrayed.

Avoid references to future events unless clearly labeled as predictions.

Writers should describe the cultural moment as if they are experiencing it in real time.

RetroVerse should feel immersive rather than retrospective.
